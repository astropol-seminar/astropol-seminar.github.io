About the series

Light carries
geometry

Polarimetry is one of astrophysics' most informative—and most underutilized—observational windows. This seminar brings together observers, theorists, and instrumentalists from around the world to for a discussion on visible light polarimetry in Astronomy.

Talks span UV, infrared, and optical polarimetry, with emphasis on connecting multi-wavelength observations to the underlying physical processes: magnetic field topology, dust alignment, scattering, and synchrotron emission.

The series is open to all. Graduate students are especially encouraged to present work in progress. The time is chosen to be accessible from the Americas, Europe, Africa, and Asia simultaneously.

Topics covered

  • Magnetic field structure in the ISM
  • Dust grain alignment mechanisms
  • Polarization in circumstellar disks
  • Exoplanet atmosphere polarimetry
  • CMB foreground polarization
  • Polarimetry of compact objects & jets
  • Next-generation instrumentation (Roman, ELTs, HWO)
  • Radiative transfer & modeling frameworks
